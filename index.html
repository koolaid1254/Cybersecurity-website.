Cybersecurity Basics

Protecting our information, accounts, and digital lives.

By Omar Anthony

What is cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, and information from unauthorized access, damage, and theft. It involves technology, rules, and the choices people make when using digital devices.

Cybersecurity matters in everyday activities such as banking, shopping, gaming, and completing schoolwork. A stolen account can expose private messages or personal information. Learning basic security habits helps people reduce these risks.

Common threats

Phishing

Phishing messages pretend to come from a trusted source. They may pressure someone to open an attachment, follow a link, or share private information.

Malware

Malware is harmful software. It can steal information or interfere with a device. Suspicious downloads and attachments can expose users to malware.

Account theft

Someone who obtains your login information may be able to access your account. Reusing a password can put other accounts at risk if that password is exposed.

Four habits for staying safer online

CISA and the FBI offer practical steps people can use to protect their accounts and information:

  1. Use strong, unique passwords. A password manager can help create and store a different password for each account.
  2. Enable multifactor authentication. MFA adds another verification step to account access. Never share your verification codes.
  3. Recognize and report phishing. Be careful with unexpected links, attachments, urgent demands, and requests for personal details.
  4. Install software updates. Updates can fix security weaknesses. Enable automatic updates when available.

Example: a suspicious school email

Imagine receiving an email saying your college account will close unless you immediately enter your password through a link. The urgent demand and request for login information are warning signs.

Instead of using the link, open the college's official website yourself or contact campus IT through a known contact method. Report the suspicious message using your school's reporting process.

Privacy and responsibility

Good security habits also include respecting other people's privacy. Only access accounts and files you have permission to use. If you discover a possible security problem at school or work, report it to the appropriate IT staff rather than testing it without authorization.
